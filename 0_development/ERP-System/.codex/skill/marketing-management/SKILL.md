# Goal
Turn a business objective into a measurable marketing plan and execution (positioning → campaigns → performance) with clear messaging, channel strategy, budget discipline, and a repeatable experimentation loop.

# When to Use
- You need to launch a product/feature and drive adoption, leads, pipeline, or revenue.
- You need a repeatable process to plan quarterly/monthly marketing and run campaigns.
- You need to improve performance (CAC, ROAS, conversion rates) using structured diagnosis and tests.

# Inputs
- Business objective: awareness, acquisition, activation, retention, expansion; targets and timeframe.
- Product context: category, differentiators, pricing, readiness, and constraints.
- Audience: ICP/personas, segments, pain points, buying committee, and regions/languages.
- Funnel baseline: current traffic, conversion rates, pipeline, CAC/ROAS, LTV, churn (if known).
- Channel constraints: budget, internal resources, brand rules, compliance (claims, regulated markets).
- Constraints:
  - Do not invent numbers; label assumptions and define how to measure.
  - Avoid storing secrets (ad accounts, API keys); reference secure storage.

# Output
- A marketing plan (1–3 pages) including:
  - ICP/segment focus and positioning
  - Messaging pillars and proof points
  - Channel mix and budget allocation
  - Campaign calendar, assets, and owners
  - Measurement plan (KPIs, tracking, dashboards)
  - Risk register and contingency plan
- A launch/campaign brief for execution teams (design/content/ops).

# Procedure
1. Clarify the objective and success metrics.
   - State the goal in numbers (even if estimated): “Increase <metric> from <baseline> to <target> by <date>.”
   - Choose 1 primary KPI and 3–5 supporting KPIs (leading/lagging).
2. Define ICP, segment, and the “why buy” narrative.
   - Pick the primary segment (don’t target everyone).
   - Write: pains, triggers, desired outcomes, and decision criteria.
   - Identify key objections and required proof (case studies, benchmarks, demos).
3. Establish positioning and messaging.
   - Positioning: category + who it’s for + unique value + why now.
   - Messaging pillars: 3–5 themes with supporting proof points.
   - Write a short message house: headline, subhead, bullets, CTA, and tone rules.
4. Select channels based on constraints and funnel stage.
   - Awareness: PR, content, social, communities, partnerships.
   - Demand gen: paid search/social, webinars, email, outbound assist.
   - Activation: onboarding, lifecycle email, in-product prompts.
   - Prioritize channels where you can measure and iterate quickly.
5. Build the campaign plan (what ships, when, who owns it).
   - Define campaign concept, offer, CTA, and landing destination.
   - Create an asset checklist: landing page, emails, ads, social, blog, demo video, sales enablement.
   - Define owners, due dates, review gates, and launch date.
6. Measurement and tracking setup (before you spend).
   - Define events and conversion points (lead, signup, activated, opportunity).
   - Ensure UTMs, attribution rules, and CRM fields are consistent.
   - Set up a dashboard with KPI definitions and update cadence.
7. Budget and forecast discipline.
   - Allocate budget by channel with expected outcomes (ranges).
   - Define guardrails: stop-loss thresholds, CPA/CAC targets, and scaling criteria.
8. Execute with weekly operating rhythm.
   - Weekly review: KPI trends, channel performance, creative learnings, pipeline feedback.
   - Update: what to double down, what to pause, what to test next.
9. Run experiments (structured).
   - Write hypotheses: “If we change X for segment Y, metric Z will improve because…”
   - Test one variable at a time where possible (message, audience, offer, landing, creative).
   - Define success criteria and minimum sample size/time window.
10. Post-campaign retro and iteration.
   - Summarize results vs baseline, insights, and next actions.
   - Convert learnings into reusable assets (best-performing messages, creatives, landing patterns).

# Verification (Acceptance Checks)
- [ ] ICP and primary segment are explicit; messaging is consistent across assets.
- [ ] KPIs are defined with baselines/targets (or clearly stated assumptions) and tracking is ready pre-launch.
- [ ] Campaign plan includes owners, dates, asset checklist, and review gates.
- [ ] Budget guardrails exist (stop-loss, scaling criteria, CPA/CAC targets).
- [ ] Weekly review cadence and experiment backlog are defined.

# Failure Modes & Recovery
- **If results are weak**: validate tracking → check funnel stage drop-off → adjust message/offer first → then channel targeting → then landing UX.
- **If attribution is unclear**: standardize UTMs and CRM source fields → define a single reporting view → re-baseline.
- **If stakeholders want “more channels”**: force prioritization → pick 1–2 primary channels + 1 test channel → backlog the rest with rationale.
- **If sales rejects lead quality**: refine ICP and qualification → adjust offers and targeting → improve sales enablement and feedback loop.
- **If missing info**: ask user “What’s the primary goal (leads/revenue/activation), who is the ICP, and what channels/budget do you have?”

# Examples
## Example A
**User request:** “Launch a new feature and drive trial signups.”
**What you do:** Define KPI (trial signups) → message house → pick channels (paid search + content + lifecycle email) → create asset checklist → set UTMs/dashboard → run 2-week tests on offer and landing copy.
**Result:** A campaign plan with assets, tracking, and an experiment loop tied to signup growth.

## Example B
**User request:** “Improve ROAS on paid social.”
**What you do:** Diagnose funnel (creative → landing → signup) → fix tracking and attribution → test new creative angles + tighter ICP targeting → define stop-loss/scaling rules → weekly iteration.
**Result:** A structured optimization plan and measurable improvements in CPA/ROAS.

# Notes (optional)
- Most failures are messaging/offer mismatch, not “channel problems”.
- Treat creative as an input to learning: ship faster, measure, and iterate.
